'Artichoke Nibbles'
from: artichoke.nib
      'Artichoke Nibbles' - recipe from VA's friend Kenny's wife, 
      at VA's 50th birthday party at Herbie's house in Oakland,
      October 1993.

      Modified over the years and here is where we are at today.

      Use marinated artichoke hearts.  Use the oil from the
      artichokes to saute the onions and garlic.

      20 oz artichokes (drained weight)
      3 large onions
      12 cloves garlic (or more)
      8 eggs
      3/4 cups crumbs (crushed saltines)
      9 oz cheese (sharp cheddar, Asiago, ??)
      1 large bunch Italian parsley
      1/2 t cayenne pepper
      1 t oregano
      1 t black pepper

      Dice onions and garlic and saute in the oil until somewhat
      soft but not browned.

      Preheat oven to 350.

      Coarsely chop artichoke hearts

      Grate cheese.

      Chop parsley.

      Beat eggs.  Add spices, crumbs, cheese, parsley.  Gently
      mix in artichokes.  Mixture will be stiff and chunky.  Let
      it be so.

      Lightly grease 10 x 15" glass baking dish.

      Spread mixture in an even layer in baking dish and bake
      for 30 minutes; should be very lightly browned on top.

      Let it cool for 20 minutes and slice into bars of the
      desired size with a sharp knife.  

      I like to make bars 1" x 2" and arrange them on a serving
      dish and decorate the top with a substantial quantity of
      paprika.  

      I get really good Paprika and other spices from Penzey's
      Spices which has a very nice web site and a wonderful
      catalog.  (800-741-7787 penzeys.com)

Butter
from: butter
10 August 2001


I used 20 grams of dried buds, 1 lb of sweet butter, and 1 gallon of
water.  I put the buds in a food processor and pulsed them a few
times for maybe 20 seconds total, to divide the stuff up without
making into dust or puree.

I boiled the water, butter, and buds together for three hours, active
boiling not just simmering.  I wanted plenty of agitation and mixing
to get extraction at the interface between oil droplets and water.

I then strained everything through a regular strainer, and I pressed
the green stuff hard to get out all the trapped butter I could.  To
check on the completeness of that operation, I cooked up the pressed
buds with another gallon of water, strained it again, and let the
water settle to see how much butter I could get off the surface.
Total residue was less than one tablespoon of butter, which I added
to the first strained liquid.  I put that all into the fridge and
when the butter layer on top was hard, I removed it and heated it
gently to melt it.  Then I poured the melted butter (which still had
lot of mushy stuff that seemed to be butter plus vegetable matter
plus incorporated water) into a milk carton and chilled it again.
After the butter was hard, I tore off the carton and discarded the
water and the mushy looking junk, and was left with 8 Oz of very nice
looking bright green clarified butter.

The notion is, the original 20 grams of buds would be 100 fairly
satisfactory doses so if I divide the butter into 100 equal parts I
can see how much of a dose one of those parts might be.  Watch this
space.

This dressing has just 13 calories and less than half a gram of fat per tablespoon, compared to 115 calories and 12 grams of fat for its traditional counterpart.

Makes about 1 cup

ACTIVE TIME: 10 minutes

TOTAL TIME: 10 minutes

EASE OF PREPARATION: Easy

1 clove garlic, crushed
1/3 cup low-fat cottage cheese
1/2 cup nonfat plain yogurt

Apple Cider Caramels From The Smitten Kitchen Cookbook
from: caramels.txt
Apple Cider Caramels From The Smitten Kitchen Cookbook 

Apple cider as I’m referring to it here,is different from both apple juice and the hard, or
alcoholic, fermented apple cider. It’s a fresh, unfiltered (it has sediment), raw apple juice. — 

4 cups (945 ml) apple cider
1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
2 teaspoons flaky sea salt, such as Maldon, or less of
      a finer one
8 tablespoons (115 grams or 1 stick) unsalted butter, cut
      into chunks
1 cup (200 grams) granulated sugar
1/2 cup (110 grams) packed light brown sugar
1/3 cup (80 ml) heavy cream
Neutral oil for the knife

 Boil the apple cider in a 3- to- 4- quart saucepan over high heat until it is reduced to a dark,
thick syrup, between 1/3 and 1/2 cup in volume. This takes about 35 to 40 minutes on my stove. Stir
occasionally.  Meanwhile, get your other ingredients in order, because you won't have time to spare
once the candy is cooking.

 Line the bottom and sides of an 8- inch straight- sided square metal baking pan with 2 long sheets
of crisscrossed parchment. Set it aside.

Stir the cinnamon and flaky salt together in a small dish.  Once you are finished reducing the apple
cider, remove it from the heat and stir in the butter, sugars, and heavy cream. Return the pot to
medium- high heat with a candy thermometer attached to the side, and let it boil until the
thermometer reads 252 degrees, only about 5 minutes.  Keep a close eye on it.  (Don’t have a candy
or deep- fry thermometer?  Have a bowl of very cold water ready, and cook the caramel until a tiny
spoonful dropped into the water becomes firm, chewy, and able to be plied into a ball.)

Immediately remove caramel from heat, add the cinnamon-salt mixture, and give the caramel several
stirs to distribute it evenly. Pour caramel into the prepared pan. Let it sit until cool and
firm—about 2 hours, though it goes faster in the fridge. Once caramel is firm, use your parchment
paper sling to transfer the block to a cutting board.  Use a well- oiled knife, oiling it after each
cut (trust me!), to cut the caramel into 1-by-1-inch squares. Wrap each one in a 4-inch square of
waxed paper, twisting the sides to close. Caramels will be somewhat on the soft side at room
temperature, and chewy/firm from the fridge.  Do ahead: Caramels keep, in an airtight container at
room temperature, for two weeks, but really, good luck with that.

(From David) I don't bother with crossing two sheets of parchment, I make an 8" by 8" liner by
clever folding of a single sheet.  I also use a pizza cutter instead of a knife.  Last time I forgot
the oil and it worked fine.  I tried concentrating 5 cups of cider, I couldn't tell a difference. 
Just for fun I computed the calories per piece (assuming 8x8 pieces) at 41.
CARROT-TOP PESTO
from: carrot-top.pesto
                        CARROT-TOP PESTO

Makes about 2/3 cup

1   cup lightly packed carrot leaves (stems removed)
6   tablespoon extra-virgin olive oil
1   large garlic clove
1/4 teaspoon kosher or fine sea salt
3   tablespoons pine nuts, toasted (see below)
1/4 cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese, preferably Parmigiano-Reggiano

To Toast The Nuts

Toasting pine nuts, almonds, walnuts, pecans, hazelnuts, cashews and
pumpkin seeds brings out their flavor. Spread the nuts or seeds in a
single layer on a rimmed baking sheet, place in a preheated 350-degree
oven and toast until fragrant and lightly browned, 5 to 10 minutes,
depending on the nut or seed. Alternatively, nuts and seeds can be
browned in a microwave. Spread in a single layer on a microwave-safe
plate and microwave on high power, stopping to stir once or twice,
until fragrant and lightly browned, 5 to 8 minutes. Watch them closely
so they don't burn.

To Make the Pesto

In a food processor, combine the carrot leaves, oil, garlic, and salt
and process until finely minced. Add the pine nuts and pulse until
finely chopped. Add the Parmesan and pulse just until combined. Taste
and adjust the seasoning. Use immediately or cover and refrigerate for
up to 2 days.

225 Recipes by Diane Morgan. Copyright 2012 by Diane Morgan. Reprinted
with permission of Chronicle Books.

The Chocolate Roll
from: chocroll
       ******* The Chocolate Roll ***


***  Recipe stolen from the public domain, and comments added by
     Joe Weisman of the Froggy Acres Church pastry kitchen

The chocolate roll is a delicate cake, about 10 by 17 inches by 1 inch
thick which is covered with flavored whipped cream and rolled into a
rough cylinder.  Proper fabrication of a chocolate roll is not difficult
for a highly skilled, dedicated, sincere and serene chef with several
years of experience dealing with the trials and frustrations which abound
in the kitchen.

Even a tyro can hope to produce chocolate rolls which are not inedible,
if he be willing to ruthlessly cull the failures (which will be most of
the first 50 chocolate rolls attempted).  The results are certainly not
worth the effort and expense required.

The Cake:

    5 eggs, separated into yolks and whites
    3 Tablespoons strong coffee
    6 ounces finest semi-sweet chocolate (this is very impor
         tant ingrediment.  Baker's German's sweet is fine,
         and some dark French cooking chocolates are finer yet
    1 cup white, refined, granular suger.  (sucrose)

Prepare a "jelly-roll" pan by greasing it, lining with a piece of
waxed paper which leave protruding at the ends a couple inches, and
greasing the paper.

Melt the chocolate in a double boiler.  When melted, add the coffee,
stir, and set aside to cool while

Whip the egg yolks till thickish and light in color (a couple minutes
with a power beater).  Add the sugar while whipping over a minute or
so, and whip another minute. Ideally this mixture should now be thicker
than heavy cream, light colored, and still slightly gritty with sugar
crystals.  Add the chocolate mixture to the yolks.  Mix till uniform
in color, or just before.

Whip the egg whites till stiff-but-not-dry.

Fold the whites and yolks together:  add about one quarter of the
whites to the yolks and mix, which will make a lighter mixture into
which to fold the rest of the whites.

Spread the cake in a roughly even layer in the prepared baking pan, and
bake at 350 degrees for about 15 minutes. The baking time is critical,
and it is difficult to determine when the cake is done. Too little time,
and the chocolate roll will be gooey.  To much, and it will be crunchy, and
it won't roll up nicely anyway.  A toothpick inserted into the middle of a
properly baked cake will come out clean,but just barely.

Remove the cake from the oven and let cool for 10 minutes.  Sprinkle a
tablespoon or so of cocoa (please don't use Nestle's any-thing, and don't
use sweetend chocolate drink mixes, what we want here is plain powdered
chocolate, dusty and bitter) over the cake and run a knife along the edges
to loosen them.  Lay a piece of waxed paper on the table and invert the pan
with cake in it over the paper.  By holding down the protruding end of waxed
paper and lifting up on the pan, you should be able to remove the pan fromp
the cake, leaving it lying on a piece of waxed paper and covered with
another.  Remove this latter mentioned carefully.  It helps to tear it off
in strips.

Whip up a cup of whipping cream till whipped.  Add the flavor of your
choice, which can be anything -- simplest is a couple tablespoons of sugar,
but we also use Cointreau, Kirsch, Creme de Cafe, Bitters, Tobasco Sauce
(truth!) and other bizarre substances.

Spread the cream over the cake, and roll it into a tube by lifting the
waxed paper under the long edge.  Move the completed roll onto a board
or suitable serving dish.  Dust with more cocoa, refrigerate if you can
stand to wait a hour or two, and eat.

Eggplant Hummus
from: eggplant.hummus
From The Curmudgeon's Home Companion,
August 2006

   Eggplant Hummus

1 large eggplant
2 cloves garlic, peeled and chopped
juice of 1 lemon
2 tablespoons of tahini (sesame paste)
1/4 cup olive oil
2 teaspoons ground cumin
2 teaspoons salt
1/2 teaspoon ground black pepper
pinch of cayenne
2 15-ounce cans garbanzos, drained and rinsed
     (or 3 1/2 cups cooked garbanzos)

1. In a hot oven, roast or broil the eggplant
until it collapses.  (Charring it gives a nice
smoky flavor.)  Allow to cool.  Scoop the flesh
out of the burnt skin into a food processor.

2. Add the garlic, lemon juice, tahini, olive oil,
cumin, salt, pepper, and cayenne.  Process until
smooth.

3. Add the garbanzos and process until smooth.

4. Taste and increase the salt, garlic, lemon, or
spices to your taste.  Serve as a dip with pita,
crackers, and raw vegetables.

Note: If you like a smoother hummus, pour into a
blender and blend until creamy.  Do it in two
batches; otherwise it will end up all over the
kitchen ceiling.

     Chicken Jocon

     Recipe modified from original in The Whole Chile Pepper Book p.124

     2 lbs skinless boneless chicken thighs
     1     onion, chopped
     6     cloves garlic, chopped
     about 1 quart water
     1/2   Cup pumpkin seeds
     1 T   oil
     18    Chiles Coban, or 4 Chipotle chiles
     2     bunches cilantro
     2     bunches scallions
     1 1/2 lbs tomatillos


     Cut up the chicken meat into chunks about 1 cubic inch
     Put into pot with onions & garlic, cover with water and simmer
     till chicken is mostly done (about 25 minutes).  Skim off 
     foam while simmering.

     When done, drain the chicken, discard the onions and garlic,
     keep the broth.

     Heat a frying pan hot, and toast pumpkin seeds for a few minutes
     till lightly toasted brown.  Add 1 T oil to pan and toast peppers.

     Use a food processor to grind the pumpkin seeds and chili peppers
     to a coarse powder.

     Use it again to reduce the cilantro, scallions and tomatillos
     to a paste.  Add some of the broth from cooking the chicken to
     make a thick sauce.

     Put the chicken, the powder, and half the sauce back into the pot,
     add enough of the broth to make a thick stew, and simmer for 15
     minutes.  Add the rest of the sauce and serve with tortillas.

MOROCCAN CARROT SALAD

1 lb sweet carrots
1 small red onion
½ preserved lemon or 1 TBSP lemon rind
¼ cup currants
1 TBSP whole cumin seeds
2 cloves garlic, peeled
Salt	
Generous pinch of cinnamon
Generous pinch of cayenne (to taste)
2 TBSP sugar
5 TBSP fresh lemon juice 
5 TBSP fruity olive oil
1 TBSP fresh mint leaves, minced
2 TBSP fresh cilantro leaves, minced
Black Pepper


Peel carrots, halve them lengthwise and cut into same-size slices.  Cook carrots until barely tender, put in ice water, drain, pat dry.  
Peel and thinly slice onion crosswise into fine slivers and add to carrots. Thinly slice preserved lemon and add to carrots along with currants.
Toast cumin in dry pan over medium heat until fragrant.  Pound in mortar and pestle, then add garlic and pinch of salt and pound until you get a fine paste.  Mix in cinnamon, cayenne, sugar, lemon juice and olive oil.  Add dressing to carrots with mint and cilantro.  Season to taste with salt and pepper.

My changes:  I chopped the onion.  I used lemon rind generously rather than preserved lemon.  I just grated garlic and did not make a paste of it.   (I put the ground cumin seeds in a bowl, grated the garlic over it and mixed in the rest of the dressing ingredients).  I used Trader Joe’s key lime juice for the fresh lemon juice.  I think you can at least halve if not eliminate the sugar that way.  I strongly suggest halving the dressing.  It is twice what you need.  Also, be generous with the cinnamon and cayenne (if you like it spicy), as well as the cumin.  I don’t think it matters how you cut the carrots, as long as they are uniform.   I also plumped the currants in hot water before adding but it’s not necessary.

      1/4 c    mayonnaise
